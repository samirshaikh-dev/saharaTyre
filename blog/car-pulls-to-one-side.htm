Car Pulls to One Side While Driving: 8 Causes and How to Fix Each

By Sahara Tyre Alignment Team • Updated October 2026 • 7 min read

A car that pulls to one side on a flat straight road is not a vague complaint. It is one of the clearest diagnostic signals in front-end geometry, and it almost always has a mechanical cause rather than a mysterious one. The trick is matching your exact symptom to the right cause, because a driver in Vapi GIDC who pays for alignment when the real problem is a low rear tyre pressure or a bent rim walks away with the same pull three weeks later.

boltDirect Answer

In roughly seven out of ten cases, a car pulling to one side is caused by either incorrect camber or incorrect toe on the front axle, both of which are fixed with a 3D laser wheel alignment costing 20 to 30 minutes at our Vapi GIDC workshop. In the remaining cases the cause is mechanical: a worn tie rod end or ball joint, sagging springs letting camber collapse, a bent rim from a pothole, or simply one tyre sitting 6 to 8 PSI below the others. Check tyre pressures cold, on a flat road, before you book any alignment work.

The 8 Causes of a Car Pulling to One Side

Cause How it presents The fix
1. Front camber out of spec Steady pull toward one side, often felt more at higher speed. Look for fast wear on one shoulder of the front tyres only. 3D laser alignment adjustment, plus checking strut top mounts and lower control arm bushes if the camber cannot be held.
2. Incorrect front toe Pull plus a steering wheel that sits off-centre while driving straight. Tread shows feathering, where one edge of each rib is rounded and the opposite edge is sharp. Toe correction with the wheel locked at centre using an interior clamp so the driver ends up with a straight wheel.
3. Uneven tyre pressure Mild pull that appears and disappears. Frequently mistaken for an alignment fault, and the most common self-inflicted cause we see in Vapi. Inflate all four to the door-jamb placard pressure while cold, then re-check the pull. A free pressure check is standard at our workshop.
4. Worn tie rod ends or ball joints Pull combined with a knocking or rattling noise over joints and speed breakers. The steering feels vague or has free play. Replace the tie rod end or ball joint first, then align. Aligning a worn joint is wasted money.
5. Sagging springs or worn struts Car sits noticeably lower on one side, rides over bumps with a thud, and pulls. Common on ageing hatchbacks and sedans. Replace spring or strut assembly on the affected corner, then re-align. The geometry cannot be corrected while the corner height is wrong.
6. Bent rim Pull that appears after a specific incident, usually a pothole or kerb strike. Often accompanied by steering vibration and a slow leak. Hydraulic rim truing for minor flange dents on steel wheels, alloy inspection for cracks, and a hydro-submersion bead leak test.
7. Worn lower control arm bushes Pull that comes and goes with braking and cornering, plus uneven inner shoulder wear on both front tyres. The car can look perfectly aligned while parked and still be wrong under load. Replace the bushes, then perform a full alignment including rear axle geometry.
8. Rear thrust angle offset The car drifts sideways and the driver holds the steering wheel crooked to travel straight. Very common on leaf-spring commercial vehicles. Rear axle alignment and thrust angle correction. On independent rear suspensions this means setting rear camber and toe properly.

Diagnose It Yourself in 5 Minutes Before You Book Anything

  1. Check pressures cold. In the morning before driving, compare all four tyres against the placard on the driver door frame. A difference of 6 PSI or more is enough to cause a pull.
  2. Swap front tyres left to right. If the pull changes direction with the tyres, the problem is the wheel or tyre, not the suspension geometry.
  3. Look at the tread pattern. Run a gloved hand across the ribs. Feathering, sharp on one edge, points to toe. One shoulder worn flat points to camber. Cupping or scalloping points to shocks and balancing.
  4. Watch the ride over joints. A knock on every speed breaker points to a worn tie rod end or sway bar link.
  5. Test at two speeds. A pull that gets stronger with speed is geometry. A vibration that peaks at one particular speed is imbalance, which is a different job entirely.

When a Pull Is Not an Alignment Problem at All

If your car pulls only when the air conditioning is on, or only when the fuel tank is nearly empty, or only at very low speeds in a tight turn, you have a different problem. Air conditioning load on a marginal engine, a tank-sling on an older vehicle, and a steering rack that binds under full lock can all create a temporary pull with perfectly correct geometry. Also check that the handbrake is fully released. Before you pay for alignment, make sure the pull is consistent at 40 km/h, 60 km/h and 80 km/h on a flat road. If it is not consistent, alignment is unlikely to be the answer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my car pulling to one side after I changed tyres? expand_more

Three things are usually responsible. First, the new tyres were fitted before the geometry was corrected, so the existing camber or toe error is now destroying the fresh tread quickly. Second, one tyre is at a different pressure from the others. Third, the rim you mounted is bent from an earlier pothole impact. Have the car aligned at the same visit as the tyre change, and check all four pressures cold.

Can a bent rim make a car pull to one side? expand_more

Yes. A rim flange knocked sideways by a pothole or kerb creates a runout that makes the tyre sit at an angle to the road. Minor flange bends on pressed steel wheels can often be hydraulically trued back. Alloy wheels are brittle and more often need replacement if the bend is severe, so we inspect them for hidden cracks before mounting anything.

How do I know if my car pull is from alignment or from low air pressure? expand_more

Check pressures cold against the door-jamb placard first. If all four match within about 2 PSI and the car still pulls on a flat straight road, it is geometry. If the pull disappears once pressures are equalised, it was pressure, and no alignment is needed.

Is a wheel alignment safe to do without replacing tyres? expand_more

Yes. Alignment is a geometry service independent of tyre condition, although a worn or damaged tyre makes accurate measurement impossible because the wheel cannot sit true on the rim. If the tyre is below the safe limit or has a sidewall bulge, we will advise replacement first.

Why does my car pull to one side only at high speed? expand_more

Above roughly 60 km/h, small geometry errors produce enough lateral force to be felt through the seat and steering wheel. At low speed the same error may be invisible. Pull that scales with speed is classic camber or toe error. Pull that peaks at one specific speed and disappears above it is more likely a wheel or tyre imbalance instead.

Do you fix steering pull for commercial trucks in Vapi? expand_more

Yes. We run heavy-duty commercial alignment systems for light commercial vehicles, tempos, and multi-axle trucks and trailers. Leaf-sprung commercial vehicles frequently develop a rear thrust angle offset that makes the vehicle dog-track, which we correct with rear axle alignment. Fleet vehicles are welcome, and we can invoice monthly with GST for transport operators.
