GIDC Fleet Tyre Service in Vapi: How to Plan a Workshop Visit

By Sahara Tyre Workshop Team • Updated October 2026 • 6 min read

A factory fleet does not buy tyres one at a time. Vehicles arrive in batches, tyre sizes repeat, and every hour in the bay is an hour out of dispatch. That changes how a workshop visit should be planned. This guide is for transport managers, plant supervisors and fleet owners in Vapi GIDC who want the work done in one organised pass instead of three unplanned trips.

Start With the Dispatch Schedule, Not the Tyre List

The most common planning mistake is to send every vehicle that needs attention on the same morning, then discover that half of them are needed for loading by noon. Work backward from dispatch instead. Group vehicles into two or three batches, and send the batch with the slackest schedule first. A truck that is parked for the day can wait for a retread; a truck loading at 11:00 AM cannot.

Share the vehicle list, tyre sizes and the job required for each vehicle before arrival. If the list includes 295/80 R22.5 steer tyres, 10.00-20 drive tyres and a set of forklift solids, that is three different stock pulls. The team can stage them in advance instead of asking the driver to wait while a size is located.

If a vehicle is being sent only for a pressure audit or a visual inspection, say so. It can often be handled in a shorter slot, which frees bay time for the vehicles that actually need tyres fitted.

Build a Simple Fleet Checklist

A useful fleet visit answers five questions per vehicle: what is the tread depth, what is the cold pressure, is the casing sound, are the dual tyres matched, and is the tyre in the right axle position? That is enough to decide whether a tyre stays, moves, gets retreaded or is scrapped.

Dual-Tyre Matching and Torque Discipline

On a tandem dual assembly, the two tyres must work as a pair. If one is more than 6 mm smaller in diameter than its partner, the taller tyre carries most of the axle load, runs hotter and wears faster. Matching is not cosmetic; it is the difference between a casing that can be retreaded and one that is scrapped early. Where a small difference exists, the taller tyre goes on the outside position because road crown loads the inner tyre more heavily.

Wheel nuts are torqued in a cross-star sequence with a calibrated wrench, not run down with an impact gun and left there. Heavy commercial studs are typically set in the 550 to 650 Nm range, and newly mounted wheels should be re-checked after the first loaded run. A wheel that loosens on the highway is a far more expensive failure than the few minutes it takes to verify torque.

Retreads: Policy, Not Guesswork

Cold precure retreads make commercial sense on drive and trailer axles when the casing is sound. They cost substantially less than a new radial and deliver a useful share of its mileage, which lowers cost per kilometre. They do not belong on the front steering axle of a high-speed truck or a passenger bus, where a sudden failure has the worst consequences.

The practical rule is to pull a casing for retreading while there is still tread left, rather than running it down to the steel. A casing that has been run flat, overheated or damaged at the bead is not a retread candidate, however good the remaining tread looks. For the maths behind the decision, read new vs remould tyres and the truck fleet maintenance protocol.

Paperwork: Get the Invoice Right the First Time

Fleet accounts are audited, so the invoice has to match the vehicle and the job. Provide the registered business name, 15-digit GSTIN, billing address, vehicle registration numbers and any purchase order reference your accounts team needs. Ask for tyre, retread and service lines to be itemised separately rather than bundled into one figure.

Keep the job slips with the vehicle until the invoice is reconciled. If a warranty claim is likely, note the tyre brand, size, DOT code and invoice number at the time of fitting. Chasing that information weeks later is slower for everyone.

A Practical Visit Plan

  • One week before: list vehicles, tyre sizes, tread depths and the job for each. Identify retread candidates and any uncommon sizes.
  • Two days before: call +91-7490052234 with the list and confirm stock, bay time and the arrival window.
  • On the day: send the batch with the slackest dispatch schedule first, and give the driver the job slip and GST details.
  • On collection: check the invoice lines, the removed tyres and the alignment or balancing report before the vehicle leaves.
  • After the visit: log the new tread depths and pressures so the next audit starts from real data, not memory.

What documents are needed for a GST tyre invoice? expand_more

Can Sahara Tyre handle on-site fleet checks inside the factory? expand_more

Fleet support can include scheduled pressure audits, tyre rotation planning, dual-tyre inspection and on-site assistance for GIDC units. Share the number of vehicles, tyre sizes and preferred shift window so the visit can be planned around production and dispatch.

Are retreads safe for fleet trucks? expand_more

Certified cold precure retreads are safe when the casing passes inspection and the tyre is fitted on a drive or trailer axle. They should not be used on front steering axles of high-speed trucks or passenger buses. Retreads cost less than new tyres and reduce cost per kilometre, provided casing quality and inflation are managed.

How often should a fleet check tyre pressure? expand_more

Pressure should be checked cold before the first dispatch of the day. A weekly audit catches slow leaks and dual-tyre mismatch before they become a roadside failure. After a long highway run, do not bleed hot tyres; check them cold the next morning.
