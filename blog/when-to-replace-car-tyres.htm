When to Replace Car Tyres: 5 Warning Signs Every Driver Must Know

By Sahara Tyre Safety Team • Updated March 2026 • 5 min read

Tyres are your vehicle's sole point of physical contact with the tarmac. Driving on severely worn or aging tyres increases braking distances exponentially and makes high-speed highway driving on NH 48 dangerous.

1. Tread Depth Worn Down to Tread Wear Indicators (TWI)

Look closely inside your tyre grooves: you will notice small horizontal raised rubber bars called Tread Wear Indicators (TWI). When the outer tread surface wears down level with these bars (1.6mm remaining), the tyre is legally bald and incapable of channeling away water during rain, causing instant aquaplaning.

2. Sidewall Bulges, Bubbles, or Deep Cuts

A bulge on the tyre sidewall indicates that the internal structural cord ply has snapped—usually from hitting a severe pothole or curb at speed. This tyre is a ticking time bomb and can suffer a sudden blowout at highway speed. Replace it immediately.

3. Age Exceeding 5 to 6 Years Regardless of Tread

Tyre rubber oxidizes and hardens over time due to UV exposure and environmental heat. Even if your car is driven infrequently and has plenty of tread remaining, tyres older than 5 years develop micro-cracks (dry rot) and lose elasticity, resulting in brittle braking performance.

4. Persistent Vibration That Wheel Balancing Doesn't Fix

If your car continues to vibrate after computerized wheel balancing, the internal steel belt within the tyre casing may have shifted or separated. Internal belt separation compromises the structural shape of the tyre and requires replacement.

5. Uneven One-Sided or Feathered Tread Wear

Severe inner or outer shoulder wear indicates prolonged alignment neglect or worn suspension tie rods. If the steel cords are visible on any edge, the tyre must be replaced and the vehicle aligned immediately.
